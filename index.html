Module 1: Accounting Foundations from Absolute Zero

Source: Principles Revision • Prerequisite: None

If you have never studied accounting before, start here. Accounting records financial transactions using equal debits and credits.

⚖️ The Fundamental Accounting Equation
Assets = Liabilities + Total Partner Capital
Assets = Liabilities + Σ (Partner Capital Accounts)

Debit vs. Credit Nature of Accounts

Debit (Dr) simply means the left side of an account. Credit (Cr) means the right side. Accounts increase on their natural home side:

Account Type Normal Balance To Increase (+) To Decrease (-) Examples in Course
Assets Debit Debit Credit Cash, Accounts Receivable, Inventory, Equipment
Expenses / Losses Debit Debit Credit Depreciation, Salaries Expense, Loss on Realization
Drawings (Withdrawals) Debit Debit Credit Partner Drawings (contra-equity account)
Liabilities Credit Credit Debit Accounts Payable, Notes Payable, Partner Loans Payable
Capital / Equity Credit Credit Debit Ahmad Capital, Dina Capital, Common Stock
Revenues & Profits Credit Credit Debit Net Profit, Sales Revenue, Interest Income
⚠️ Essential Distinction: Profit vs. Revenue vs. Drawings
  • Revenue: The total gross money earned from selling goods or services.
  • Profit (Net Income): The net profit left after deducting all business expenses from revenue (Profit = Revenue - Expenses).
  • Withdrawal / Drawings: Cash or assets taken out of the business by partners for personal use. A drawing is not an operating expense of the firm.

Module 3: Nature, Legal Definitions & Characteristics

Source: Definitions of Partnership & Characteristics

A partnership is formed when two or more persons bind themselves to contribute money, property, or industry to a common fund with the intention of dividing the profits among themselves.

The 8 Core Legal Characteristics

1. Ease of Formation

Created by mutual agreement without complicated state charters.

2. Separate Legal Personality

The partnership entity exists separately from the personal affairs of the partners.

3. Mutual Agency

Every partner is an authorized agent who can legally bind the firm in contracts.

4. Unlimited Liability

General partners are personally liable for unpaid firm debts using personal wealth.

5. Co-ownership of Property

Contributed assets become joint partnership property, not personal property.

6. Co-ownership of Profits

All partners share profits and losses per their partnership agreement.

7. Limited Life

Any change in partners (admission, withdrawal, death) dissolves the partnership.

8. Consensual Transfer

No person can become a partner without the unanimous consent of all partners.

Module 4: Partnership Formation (Brand New Firm)

Source: Scenarios 1: Assumptions 1, 2, 3 & Golden Co
📌 The 3 Cardinal Rules of Partnership Formation
  1. Fair Market Value / Agreed Value: Non-cash assets must be recorded at their agreed fair market values at the date of formation, never at historical cost.
  2. NO Accumulated Depreciation Carried Forward: Previous accumulated depreciation from old books is discarded. The new firm records property at agreed net value as its new cost.
  3. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts IS Retained: Accounts Receivable is recorded at gross face value, and the agreed Allowance for Uncollectible Accounts is credited to preserve customer records while displaying net cash realizable value.

Module 6: Partnership Operations & Profit Allocation

Source: Illustrations 1, 4 & Example 1

Partnership profits are distributed using the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account.

📜 Order of Priority in Profit Allocation
  1. Interest on Capital: Compensates partners for differing amounts of invested capital.
  2. Partners' Salaries: Compensates partners for personal management and labor time.
  3. Partners' Commissions / Bonuses: Performance incentives based on net earnings.
  4. Interest on Drawings: Charged to partners who withdraw funds, credited back to the firm's appropriation account.
  5. Transfers to Reserve: Statutory or agreed reserves.
  6. Remaining Divisible Profit / Loss: Divided strictly according to the agreed Profit and Loss Sharing Ratio. If no ratio is agreed upon, profits and losses are shared equally (50:50) by law.

Module 7: Managerial Commissions & Prior-Period Errors

Source: Illustration 4 (Partners A, B, and C)

One of the most heavily tested areas in university accounting is calculating partner commissions when agreements state "commission after charging..."

📐 The Two Commission Rules
1. Commission BEFORE charging such commission:
Commission = Net Profit Base × Rate
2. Commission AFTER charging his OWN commission:
Commission = Net Profit Base × [Rate / (100 + Rate)]
For a 10% rate: Base × (10 / 110)

Interactive Step-by-Step Solver: Illustration 4

Net profit reported: $162,000. Overlooked Plant Depreciation: $18,000. Reserve: 10%. Salary A: $2,000/mo. Commissions: A = 10% Net Profit; B = 10% after A's commission; C = 10% after A, B, and his own commission.

Step 1: Depreciation Adjustment

Operating expenses like depreciation must be deducted before computing partnership appropriations:

Adjusted Net Profit = $162,000 - $18,000 (Depreciation) = $144,000
Step 2: Reserve & Salaries
  • Transfer to Reserve (10%): $144,000 × 10% = $14,400
  • Salary to A: $2,000 × 12 months = $24,000
  • Interest on Capital (10%): A: $6,000, B: $8,000, C: $5,000 (Total = $19,000)
  • Interest on Drawings (10% on withdrawals): A: $400, B: $600, C: $300 (Total Credit = $1,300)
Step 3: Calculating Partner Commissions
  • Commission A (10% on Net Profit):
    $144,000 × 10% = $14,400
  • Commission B (10% after charging A's commission):
    Per course formula: $13,091
  • Commission C (10% after A, B, and HIS OWN commission):
    Remaining Base = $144,000 - $14,400 - $13,091 = $116,509
    Commission C = $116,509 × (10 / 110) = $10,592
Step 4: Divisible Profit Distribution (5:3:2)

Total Credits ($144,000 + $1,300) - Total Debits ($19,000 + $24,000 + $14,400 + $14,400 + $13,091 + $10,592) = $49,817

Allocated per deed: First $30,000 equally ($10,000 each), remainder $19,817 in ratio 5:3:2:

  • A: $10,000 + (19,817 × 5/10) = $19,909
  • B: $10,000 + (19,817 × 3/10) = $15,945
  • C: $10,000 + (19,817 × 2/10) = $13,963

Module 8: Fixed vs. Fluctuating Capital Systems

Source: Illustrations 2 and 3
Swipe ↔ to view columns
Feature Fixed Capital System Fluctuating Capital System
Accounts Maintained per Partner Two: Capital a/c + Current a/c One: Capital a/c only
What is Recorded in Capital a/c? Only original investment, permanent capital additions, or permanent capital withdrawals. Everything: initial capital, drawings, salary, interest on capital, and profit/loss share.
What is Recorded in Current a/c? All regular operational items: salary, interest, profit/loss share, and drawings. Not used (no current account exists).
Balance Sheet Presentation Shows Capital a/c (constant) ± Current a/c balance. Shows fluctuating Capital a/c ending balance.

Module 9: Dissolution (Bonus vs. Revaluation Method)

Core University Curriculum Topic

Bonus Method: Total Agreed Capital = Total Contributed Capital. No artificial goodwill or asset revaluation is recognized on the balance sheet.

Bonus to Old Partners = New Partner Investment - (Total Agreed Capital × New % Share)

Module 10: Partnership Liquidation (Winding Up)

Core University Curriculum Topic

The 4 strict legal steps in liquidation:

  1. Realization: Sell non-cash assets for cash and record gain/loss.
  2. Division of Gain/Loss: Allocate realization gain or loss strictly in the P/L ratio.
  3. Payment of Liabilities: Pay outside creditors in full before partner capital.
  4. Distribution of Cash: Distribute remaining cash to partners strictly according to their positive capital balances.
