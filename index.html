Module 1: Accounting Foundations from Absolute Zero

Source: Principles Revision • Prerequisite: None

Accounting tracks business value using equal double-entry transactions.

⚖️ The Fundamental Accounting Equation
$$\text{Assets} = \text{Liabilities} + \sum (\text{Partner Capital})$$

Debit vs. Credit Nature of Accounts

Debit (Dr) = Left side. Credit (Cr) = Right side.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls: Revenue vs. Profit vs. Drawings

Revenue: Gross money earned from sales.

Profit (Net Income): What is left after deducting business expenses ($\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses}$).

Drawings: Money removed by partners for personal living expenses. Drawings are not expenses of the business.

Module 2: Nature, Legal Definitions & Characteristics

Source: Partnership Definitions & Characteristics

A partnership is a legal relation formed by contract between two or more competent persons uniting property, money, or labor for joint profit.

Core Legal Characteristics

  • Mutual Agency: Any partner acts as an authorized agent able to bind the firm.
  • Unlimited Liability: Partners can lose personal assets if business assets cannot satisfy creditors.
  • Limited Life: Any change in partners dissolves the legal contract.
  • Co-ownership of Property: Contributed assets belong to the firm jointly.

Module 3: Partnership Formation (New Firm)

Source: Scenarios 1: Assumptions 1, 2, 3
📌 Key Accounting Principles for Formation
  1. Agreed / Fair Market Value: Non-cash assets must be recorded at agreed value, never at historical cost.
  2. No Old Accumulated Depreciation: Previous depreciation is discarded; agreed value becomes the new historical cost.
  3. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts Is Retained: Gross receivables are debited; allowance is credited to show net collectible value.

Module 5: Partnership Operations & Profit Allocation

Source: Illustrations 1, 4 & Example 1

Partnership profits are distributed using the Profit & Loss Appropriation Account.

Interactive Step-by-Step Problem Solver (Illustration 4)

Net profit: $162,000. Overlooked depreciation: $18,000. Reserve: 10%. A salary: $2,000/mo. Commissions: A = 10% NP; B = 10% after A's commission; C = 10% after A, B, and his own commission.

Step 1: Depreciation Adjustment
$$\text{Adjusted Net Profit} = \$162,000 - \$18,000 = \mathbf{\$144,000}$$
Step 2: Reserve & Salaries
  • Reserve (10%): $\$144,000 \times 10\% = \mathbf{\$14,400}$
  • A's Salary: $\$2,000 \times 12 = \mathbf{\$24,000}$
  • Interest on Capital: A: $\$6,000$, B: $\$8,000$, C: $\$5,000$ (Total $= \mathbf{\$19,000}$)
  • Interest on Drawings: A: $\$400$, B: $\$600$, C: $\$300$ (Total Credit $= \mathbf{\$1,300}$)
Step 3: Calculating Partner Commissions
  • Commission A: $\$144,000 \times 10\% = \mathbf{\$14,400}$
  • Commission B: Course formula: $\mathbf{\$13,091}$
  • Commission C: Base $= \$144,000 - \$14,400 - \$13,091 = \$116,509$ $$\text{Commission C} = \$116,509 \times \frac{10}{110} = \mathbf{\$10,592}$$
Step 4: Divisible Profit Distribution (5:3:2)

Remaining profit = $49,817

  • A: $10,000 + (19,817 \times 5/10) = \mathbf{\$19,909}$
  • B: $10,000 + (19,817 \times 3/10) = \mathbf{\$15,945}$
  • C: $10,000 + (19,817 \times 2/10) = \mathbf{\$13,963}$

Module 8: Dissolution (Bonus vs. Revaluation Method)

Bonus Method: Total Agreed Capital = Total Contributed Capital. No goodwill recognized.

$$\text{Bonus} = \text{Investment} - (\text{Total Agreed Capital} \times \% \text{ Share})$$

Module 9: Partnership Liquidation (Winding Up)

1. Realize non-cash assets $\rightarrow$ 2. Allocate gain/loss in P/L ratio $\rightarrow$ 3. Pay outside creditors $\rightarrow$ 4. Distribute cash according to positive capital balances.
